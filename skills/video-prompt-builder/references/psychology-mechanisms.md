# Psychology Mechanisms — Universal Scroll-Stop Arsenal

16 mechanisms. Use these as the spine of every video plan. Each has a tactical move and a documented source.

## The Three Phases

```
PHASE A (0–3s)     — THE STOP            (neurological, subcortical)
PHASE B (3–8s)     — THE BRIDGE          (curiosity debt, cognitive)
PHASE C (after)    — THE RESIDUE         (loop, parasocial, earworm)
```

Each video must solve all three.

---

## PHASE A — The 0–3 Second Stop

### Mechanism 1 — Orienting Response (Sokolov 1963)
The thumb-stop is involuntary, subcortical, fires before conscious awareness. Triggered by: unexpected motion, high-contrast luminance change, stimulus-context mismatch, biologically relevant targets (face, eye, threat, sex, food, infant).

**Tactical moves:**
- Frame 1 must contain a luminance/color anomaly vs. what the feed just showed
- Motion in frame 1 (even a subtle push-in or head tilt beats a clean static)
- Visible eye contact with lens within 0.25s loads the FFA

### Mechanism 2 — Fusiform Face Area (FFA) Hijack
Face detection fires at 50-75ms. Face-to-lens is neurologically un-ignorable. Attention modulates FFA output — the face pulls the attention system toward the clip regardless of "choice."

**Tactical moves:**
- Frame 1 = human/anthropomorphic face, eyes locked to camera, mouth mid-word
- Low angle + too-close-to-lens (~30-40cm apparent distance) triggers intimacy geometry
- Non-face alternative: infant-face schema (round, big-eyed, high-forehead proportions)

### Mechanism 3 — Pattern Interrupt vs. Feed Baseline
Pattern interrupts raise engagement ~3x; TikTok internal data shows 41% completion-rate lifts when hook patterns are layered. You interrupt the scroll rhythm, not the previous video.

**Tactical moves:**
- Know the feed baseline (native TikTok cold look). Your frame 1 must violate it.
- Cold audience → visual anomaly (scale, motion, color). Warm audience → identity anomaly ("what did HE just say?").

### Mechanism 4 — Visual + Text + Verbal Triad
Paddy Galloway's 3.3B-views analysis: three channels racing to land the stop in <1s. Text is the fallback hook when audio is off (50%+ of feed views are muted).

**Tactical moves:**
- First text on screen by ms 200. Max 5 words. All caps or Hormozi-weight bold.
- Text states the curiosity payload, NOT the topic. Bad: "Crypto trading tips." Good: "I lost $200K in 40 seconds."
- Hormozi pattern: text promises the loop, audio opens it.

## PHASE B — The 3–8 Second Bridge

### Mechanism 5 — Loewenstein Information Gap (1994)
Curiosity tracks **perceived proximity to resolution**, not distance. Close gaps pull hardest. Far gaps produce frustration → bounce.

**Tactical moves:**
- Seconds 2-3: state the gap explicitly. "Most traders lose money for one reason — and it's not the one you think."
- The "not the one you think" phrase is load-bearing — it tells the brain the answer exists and is reachable.
- Gap must feel 20-40s away, not 5 minutes. Never open a gap you don't visibly move toward by second 8.

### Mechanism 6 — Zeigarnik Effect (1927)
Interrupted tasks recalled ~2x better than completed. Unclosed loops squat in working memory until resolved — you are literally occupying the viewer's RAM.

**Tactical moves:**
- **Stack loops.** Loop A opens at 2s → loop B opens at 6s → A partially resolves while B is still running → loop C opens. MrBeast stacks 3 open loops simultaneously.
- **Midway hooks** at seconds 25-35 — this is where the second drop-off cliff hits. A pattern interrupt here (sound effect, cut, angle change) recovers the back half.

### Mechanism 7 — Crazy Progression (MrBeast)
Instead of linear setup ("today I will X"), flash non-linear milestones in the first 15s — day 1, day 3, day 7. Viewer is emotionally invested in trajectory before the "real" video starts.

**Tactical moves:**
- Seconds 3-6: flash-cut a glimpse of the payoff (transformation, reveal, result). Not giving it away — anchoring what they're staying for.
- Frontload visual-variable changes. Hormozi cadence: angle or b-roll change every 1.5-3s in the bridge period.

### Mechanism 8 — Cognitive Strain as Retention Glue
Controlled confusion holds attention. Brain can't parse immediately but senses resolution is near → stays to close the ambiguity. Overdo → bounce. Underdo → no stickiness.

**Tactical moves:**
- Introduce ONE unexplained element in 3-8s (object, sound, visual you'll explain later).
- Crypto example: chart visible in corner, never referenced — users rewatch to read it.

## PHASE C — Post-View Residue

### Mechanism 9 — Loop Architecture (Colin & Samir data)
Shorts that loop end→start average **143% more views** and higher subscribe rates. The loop exploits Zeigarnik + sends "rewatch" as high-retention signal to the algorithm.

**Tactical moves:**
- Final frame visually mirrors frame 1 (seamless rewatch).
- Final line is a **question** or a **claim that reframes the opening** — forces a mental re-run even if the viewer scrolls.

### Mechanism 10 — Parasocial Imprint
Engineered through: eye-to-lens contact, handheld intimacy, "unpolished" setting (bedroom, car, kitchen). Feeling: "this person is talking to me." Converts to rewatch, save, share.

**Tactical moves:**
- Handheld >>> gimbal for intimacy content. Micro-jitter reads as "human presence."
- Shoot in backstage settings. Polish kills parasocial.
- Second-person singular ("you" not "you guys").

### Mechanism 11 — Earworm Mechanics for Video
Short + repetitive + unresolved = sticks in the brain after viewing.

**Tactical moves:**
- Build a 3-second signature intro/outro that recurs across the creator's catalog (sonic branding).
- End with an unresolved musical or tonal beat. Unresolved = replay.

## Ape-Brain Exploit Tactics

### Mechanism 12 — Tribal Signaling (In/Out-Group)
Humans evolved as coalition animals. "I'm your tribe" fires affiliation; "they're the enemy" fires threat/defense. Both hold attention.

**Tactical moves:**
- Frame 1 language: insider lexicon. For CT: "anon," "ngmi," "based," ticker syntax.
- Enemy framing: "VCs want you to believe X." Enemy doesn't have to be real — visible.
- Status hierarchy reveal: "If you're still using [low-status thing], stop."

### Mechanism 13 — Ego-Complicity (The Insider Frame)
Make the viewer feel they are being let in on something most people won't get. Content becomes part of their identity. They watch, save, share as self-reinforcement.

**Tactical moves:**
- "99% of people scrolling past this won't understand it. You will." (second 1, text).
- "I shouldn't be saying this publicly but —" (trust-giving frame, reciprocity trigger).
- "If you know, you know."

### Mechanism 14 — Variable-Ratio Reward (Inside the Clip)
Platforms use variable-ratio reinforcement in the scroll. Replicate it INSIDE the video. Make payoff moments unpredictable.

**Tactical moves:**
- Don't put good stuff at predictable rhythm. Scatter reveals, laughs, punches at irregular intervals.
- This is why Hormozi-style rapid color highlights + SFX + zoom punches work — micro-rewards on variable schedule.

### Mechanism 15 — Pre-Verbal Triggers
Route through amygdala/VTA faster than language parsing. Any in frame 1 buys time.

**Tactical moves:**
- Money on screen (cash, wallet, big number) — primal reward signal
- Food close-up — vagal activation, holds gaze
- Threat cue (fast motion toward lens, shattered glass) — freezes orienting response
- Use sparingly. Overuse habituates and platforms flag gimmicks.

### Mechanism 16 — Status Anxiety
"You're behind." "You're missing it." "While you were doing X, they were doing Y." Activates social-comparison circuitry. Uncomfortable → can't scroll → must hear resolution.

**Tactical moves:**
- Time-anchored loss aversion: "If you're not doing [X] by [date], you'll be priced out."
- Peer-superiority cue: "My friend made $X in 30 days doing this. I ignored him for a year."

## Awareness Levels (Eugene Schwartz, 1966)

Frame 1 must match where the viewer is. Mismatched awareness = instant scroll.

1. **Unaware** → lead with emotional/identity hook (not with product)
2. **Problem-aware** → name their pain in their own words
3. **Solution-aware** → differentiate your approach
4. **Product-aware** → specific proof
5. **Most aware** → offer / CTA

Pitching product-aware copy to unaware audience = instant bounce. Know which level your audience is at before Frame 1.

## Anti-Patterns (Retention Killers)

- **Slow cold open** ("hey guys today I'm going to talk about…") — instant death
- **Logo/intro/title card** — catastrophic. Viewer gives attention to humans, not brands
- **Unresolved gap too big/far** — produces frustration, user flees
- **Too-clean production** — kills parasocial imprint. Polish = distance.
- **Wrong awareness level** — product pitch to unaware audience
- **Predictable reward rhythm** — brain predicts, disengages
- **Voice-only, muted-by-default video with no text hook** — 50%+ muted on first pass
- **Trying to be broad** — broad = no tribe = no identity trigger
- **Single loop that closes at 15s in a 45s video** — nothing holds the back half

---

## The Highest-Leverage Principles (Operate From These)

1. Frame 1 is neurological, not editorial
2. Stopping creates a debt — pay in the first 8s with an opened loop that feels closeable
3. Stack loops; never close them all at once
4. Visual + Text + Verbal, simultaneous
5. Parasocial > Production
6. Encode the tribe in the first frame
7. Variable-ratio rewards inside the clip
8. Loop the end to the beginning
9. Pattern-interrupt every 15-25s; hard-interrupt at 25-35s
10. The enemy matters as much as the promise
